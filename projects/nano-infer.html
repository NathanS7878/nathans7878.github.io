nano-infer

A from-scratch single-GPU LLM inference engine with custom CUDA kernels — transformer inference without generate(), vLLM, TensorRT-LLM, or FlashAttention.

CUDAC++PythonPyTorch

9.40× HuggingFace generate() throughput at batch 32 · 78.3% of peak memory bandwidth on Qwen2.5-1.5B · 165 parity tests.

The problem

Token generation on a GPU is memory-bandwidth-bound, not compute-bound. The HuggingFace baseline made that obvious before a line of the engine existed: inter-token latency sat at ~51 ms whether the batch was 1 or 32, because every decode step pays to stream the whole model out of VRAM. Most of the win comes from moving less data and wasting less time between kernels, not from doing less math.

The rule the project holds itself to: HuggingFace is used for weights and tokenizer download only. The forward pass, the sampling loop, and the cache are mine. No vLLM, TensorRT-LLM, FlashAttention, or xformers.

What was built

  • Correct first. A full forward pass from raw safetensors, every component bit-identical to HuggingFace eager, token-for-token on the reference prompts. Correctness gates every optimization.
  • Paged KV cache and continuous batching — 3.8× fewer cache slots held, 4.4% fragmentation, and 1.55× on a request stream as sequences join and leave the batch mid-flight.
  • Four custom CUDA kernels — fused RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE, and an online-softmax decode attention kernel. SwiGLU and RoPE landed within 1% of a ceiling predicted by counting bytes before writing any code.
  • CUDA-graph decode — the eager decode step turned out to be host-bound (~3,200 dispatches per step). Capturing the step as a graph made decode ~5× faster at short context.
  • INT8/INT4 weight quantization with a fused dequantize-matmul kernel.

On the larger Qwen2.5-1.5B the graphed batch-1 step reaches 350.6 GB/s — 78.3% of the card's peak. INT8 weights decode 1.61× faster than bf16 at batch 1 for +0.17% perplexity, inside the measurement error.

What I got wrong

The decode attention kernel missed its predicted ceiling by 10×, reaching only 11% of peak bandwidth. The cause wasn't the load path: with grouped-query attention, seven query heads share each KV head, and the kernel launched one block per query head — so the same KV rows were read seven times. Against the bytes it actually issued, it was already at 76% of peak. Regrouping so one block serves all seven heads made the kernel 2.60× faster at identical numerical error.

Limitations

  • No vLLM comparison. vLLM has no Windows wheels and its source build fails on this machine. Rather than quote someone else's hardware, the row is left empty.
  • Quantization is a trade, not a free win. The fused dequant kernel loses to cuBLAS from batch 4 up, badly at batch 32. INT4 costs ~18–21% perplexity with no calibration pass.
  • Decode attention is still at 30% of peak. Split-K (flash-decoding) is the named next fix and isn't implemented.
  • One GPU, two small models. Everything is an 8 GB RTX 3070; crossover points are properties of this hardware.
